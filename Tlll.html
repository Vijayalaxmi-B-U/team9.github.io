Algorithm-Driven Urban & Industrial Waste Optimization

Collaboration Overview

Rapid urban growth and industrial expansion generate large volumes of mixed waste, including recyclable, non-recyclable, and hazardous materials. Traditional waste handling methods rely heavily on manual segregation and fixed collection schedules, leading to inefficiencies and safety risks.

This collaboration integrates intelligent waste classification with industrial-scale waste collection and routing to create a unified, algorithm-driven waste management system for smart cities.

Intelligent Waste Classification & Recycling System (Sushant)

This system focuses on automatic identification and classification of waste using sensor data and algorithmic analysis. The goal is to efficiently separate recyclable, non-recyclable, and hazardous waste at an early stage.

Early classification improves recycling efficiency, reduces human error, and prevents hazardous materials from entering general waste streams.

Smart Industrial Waste Management (Vaishnavi)

This system manages large-scale industrial waste through automated monitoring, prioritization, and optimized collection routing. Industrial zones and roads are modeled using graph-based representations.

Real-time bin data enables timely waste collection, overflow prevention, and reduced fuel consumption.

How the Collaboration Improves City Efficiency

By integrating waste classification with industrial waste routing, the city gains a complete view of waste generation and disposal needs. This unified approach significantly improves operational efficiency.

  • Early waste segregation reduces downstream processing cost
  • Hazardous waste is identified and prioritized immediately
  • Optimized collection routes reduce fuel usage and emissions
  • Overflow prevention improves hygiene and public health
  • Automation reduces dependency on manual monitoring

Overall, the collaboration results in cleaner urban spaces, safer industrial operations, and more sustainable waste management.

Algorithms Used in the Collaboration

  • Pattern Matching (KMP / Brute Force) – Hazardous waste detection
  • Sorting Algorithms (Merge / Quick Sort) – Rank waste and bins by urgency
  • Hash Tables – Map waste categories to disposal methods
  • Priority Queue – Handle hazardous waste first
  • AVL / Red-Black Trees – Efficient bin data management
  • Dijkstra’s Algorithm – Optimize waste collection routes
  • Graph Models – Represent industrial zones and road networks
